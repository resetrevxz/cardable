# Rarity opening intros — quality and refraction revisions

Basic through Exotic run after the foil tear/fall, before the reserved card is mounted. Each unresolved card in a multi-card pack gets its own rarity sequence. Selection uses the already-reserved card: rendering never pulls again, changes a serial, consumes another pack or grants another reward. Ascendant and higher tiers retain their existing presentation.

## Renderer and handoff

Legendary begins with rising golden embers and continuous molten side contours, with independently advected noise heat and curling wisps. A tall gold prism approaches and slowly turns into alignment. Its metallic facets, internal crystal detail, narrow light core and molten rim refer to the supplied golden-outline/crystalline weapon image without reproducing a weapon. Three one-second illumination passes move in mirrored halves: center to edges, edges to center, then a final center-out crystallization that fills the screen. Light catches individual facets with beveled edges and small local highlights; there are no rectangular sweep masks. The peak seals, then resolves into a quiet gold crystal background at the card handoff.

Unusual and Double Super Rare share an original refractive glass/gel material inspired by the supplied purple reference. Long gently warped seams cross irregular light-catching facets. Cached noise folds, beveled caustics, dark pockets and a separately advected highlight layer provide depth. A deliberate elongated corner sparkle anchors the composition. Sparse purple/white beams gain speed and density, leaving refracted echoes before the surface fills with purple-white. The 140 ms lock freezes travel at the peak; the short release goes straight into the card flip. Double Super Rare preserves a blue left half and gold right half throughout, including the final blue-white/gold-white saturation. Continuous beam geometry is tinted separately as it crosses the center seam.

`src/data/rarities.js` owns the palettes, `sections: [{id, ms}]` and `handoff: {flipMs:400, fadeMs:160}`. `src/fx/rarity-intro.js` uses one lazy bounded opaque canvas, cached glow sprites and a cached 512 px layered-noise cloud atlas. Seeds are derived from reserved instance IDs, independent of gameplay randomness. There are no external assets, dependencies, network requests or sound.

`src/fx/refraction-intro.js` is a material painter called by that same controller, with no private timer, RAF or visible canvas. It bakes 512 px body/highlight atlases once per palette using warped noise and irregular facets; the shared cache is bounded to seven palettes. Fixed pane/glint/ray arrays are seeded from the reserved instance. Subsequent frames use image blits and bounded paths, not pixel generation. Slow glass advection/seams are composited at 30 Hz into a cached field capped at 640 px on its longer dimension; beams, sparkles, saturation and handoff still draw on every shared frame. The visible refraction canvas is capped at 1.5 million pixels on High and 1.3 million on Medium. Very Low omits the atlas. Static accessibility/Low alternatives retain the glass and split palettes but disable beams, sparkle pulsing, light travel and impact flashes. Live quality/Mono changes refresh the material without restarting the reserved reveal.

`src/fx/gilded-intro.js` follows the same painter pattern. It owns bounded instance-seeded 192-facet geometry, 36 embers, cached bronze/hot crystal fields, one 128×256 periodic noise heat material, and up to eight glow sprites. Flame advection and crystal lighting fields are composited at 30 Hz at a maximum 640 px long dimension; the main prism, its approach, particles and handoff paint on the shared scheduler. Visible gilded canvases are bounded to 1.5/1.3 million pixels on High/Medium.

The optional descriptor `backdrop:{enabled:true,exitMs:450}` retains a static field on a separate canvas behind the card, with no private animation loop or recurring paint while reading. `beginHandoff` mounts it before the foreground overlay fades. `exitBackdrop`/`updateBackdrop` fade it during collection or discard; `stop` clears both canvases on reset, replay or the next card. Resize and live Mono/quality changes repaint it. `restoreBackdrop` rebuilds it deterministically from the reserved instance on reload without restarting the intro. The end of the foreground intro still emits `opening:introEnd` once.

The controller exposes `start`, `update`, `beginHandoff`, `updateRelease`, `stop` and `resize`. At main-intro completion, the opening controller mounts the card back at its final position and enters `flipping` directly. The last effect frame remains above it and fades over the first 160 ms (112 ms Fast). The shared opening scheduler advances both; `stop` hides the canvas and emits `opening:introEnd` once. Resets stop immediately. `opening:introStart` and `opening:introSection` remain available. The intro has no separate RAF or Medium 30 Hz throttle; the shared FPS limit controls drawing. Only slow cloud advection is composited into a cached 384/512 px Medium/High field at 30 Hz; camera movement, rings, stars and release still paint on every shared frame. Existing floating pack receipts and collection toasts are visually suppressed during the intro/flip while their clocks and rewards remain intact.

Mono converts every material, background and highlight to grayscale. Reduced motion uses an 800 ms restrained static fade and the existing card crossfade, without particle travel, zoom or flashes. Low uses a static intro capped at 1200 ms; Very Low uses a 600 ms static intro. High and Medium use a maximum 2.0/1.3 million pixels and DPR 1.5; resize redraws the current state without restarting. Hidden tabs stop the shared scheduler and resume without counting the hidden interval.

Reload during `pendingReveal` immediately restores the same front card and final variant with Keep/Delete. No intro is replayed. Keep/Delete remain unavailable during the ceremony, flip and metadata/variant readiness.

## Mythical — Crimson Clock

Normal: cave 5000 → tip/fracture 1800 → fall 1200 → impact 1000 → underwater side shot 4500 → overhead transition 2200 → omen 3000 → clock/title 4600 → rupture 3300 → explosion 500 → release 500 → flip 400 ms. The intro is 27,600 ms, and the total is 28,000 ms. Fast scales it to 19,600 ms. The existing pack charge/tear, metadata and variant readiness are additional and unchanged. Each section's boundaries come from the descriptor, including the spatial camera transitions, so changing a section length does not break subsequent shots.

`mythical-scene.js` owns a private native WebGL2 scene and no timer or RAF. It is lazy-created only for an animated Mythical intro. Irregular stone meshes use a seeded cached 256 px roughness/normal material; seven-sided ruby crystals have distinct facets, internal absorption/light and a ceiling-pivoted hero. A half-resolution mirrored-camera pass feeds animated water normals and impact rings. Analytic droplets/splash crown, fracture chips and rooted moving tendrils share the same shot clock. Medium uses fewer background crystals/particles. The visible scene is capped at 1.5 million pixels on High and 975,000 on Medium, at DPR 1.5; reflection and bloom targets are half resolution. Shader programs, buffers, textures and targets are deleted when the spatial scene ends, when switching to static motion, or when stopping. Context loss or unavailable WebGL falls back to Canvas at the current timeline position without touching the reservation.

`mythical-intro.js` composites that scene into the existing controller's Canvas2D overlay, then crossfades the overhead water-star into the ritual. It caches a 384 px layered ruby smoke material, a 128×256 flame material, bounded instance-seeded particles/rim parts/shards and original vector letterforms. The supplied clock reference informs the broken beveled rim, engraved marks, rotating frames, three hands and recoil; its countdown/audio/application are not imported. The title first reads MYTHICAL, then corrupts into symbols and sliced echoes before breaking. One local pulsing star accelerates into a single full-screen explosion flash; reduced/static alternatives omit travel, glitching, pulses and flash.

The retained-background dispatch selects Legendary or Mythical's painter. Mythical caches a 640×480 base with a dark center and dim rim remnants, then composites slow edge smoke/embers at no more than 10 Hz. It releases the 3D scene and pauses on hidden tabs, blur, activity sleep or static motion/quality. The opening subscriber stays awake only while the backdrop needs animation or exit. Recovery reconstructs the seed-derived backdrop at its initial quiet phase and exposes the same front/variant immediately; it does not persist the ephemeral smoke phase. Static alternatives restore pixel-identically at a fixed viewport/palette. The existing 450 ms decision exit still clears the backdrop before the next reveal.

Mythical and Exotic's intros and flips use analytic elapsed poses rather than the physics delta cap, preserving their lengths across slow frames. The existing visibility handler resets the clock on pause/resume, so hidden time is excluded. Other rarity clocks retain their prior behavior. Section/start/end events and exact-once card/reward semantics are unchanged.

## Exotic — interrupted light and stellar flight

Normal is 19,300 ms of intro plus a 400 ms flip, or 19.7 seconds after the foil tear. Fast is 13,510 + 280 ms (13.79 seconds), with a seven-second acceleration. Section durations and the palette remain editable in the rarity descriptor. The existing three-second charge, tear, metadata and variant readiness are additional. Exotic borrows Basic's actual corner/spread/peak painter and neutral palette for the first 880 ms, interrupting it before retreat or card mounting; the intentional 520 ms blackout is the only black shot.

`exotic-intro.js` uses native Canvas2D and the shared reveal timeline, with no private loop, dependency, audio or network asset. Instance-seeded stars have perspective depth, local twinkle and a continuous velocity integral. Their quadratic acceleration stretches them into long violet/pink/pale beams, with curved travel, edge light, restrained shake and layered shutter trails. A 420 ms analytic deceleration and fading ghost exposure lead into galaxy ignition. Two cached 768 px layered-noise materials produce gas, spiral ridges and broken dark dust lanes. Three seeded 1024 px stellar atlases carry 5800 points, orbiting at different speeds around a tilted disk; selective live sparkles, golden glints, a compact pale core and shooting stars provide depth. Caches replace per-frame drawing of thousands of circles while retaining density. High/Medium visible canvases are capped at 1.5 million/975,000 pixels and DPR 1.5; Medium reduces foreground stars.

The galaxy fades over 1200 ms while the same seeded perspective starfield continues without a position reset. Its center dims gradually for card readability. At the end, the exact quiet field is already present; the controller retains it before the overlapping 160 ms foreground fade. The retained field slowly drifts/twinkles at at most 10 Hz and occasionally has a faint edge shooting star. It freezes on blur, hidden tabs, activity sleep and static policies, and clears after the 450 ms Keep/Delete exit or before the next card. Recovery creates only the starfield, skipping galaxy-material preparation, and exposes the saved front/variant without replay or another reward. Its ephemeral animation resets to the same deterministic quiet phase.

Mono converts every gas/star/glint/background channel. Reduced motion, Low and Very Low use the existing 800/1200/600 ms short static alternatives, with no shake, star travel or galaxy burst. They still use the brief flip/crossfade and keep the restrained static starfield. Live resizing reframes the complete galaxy inside landscape or portrait, without restarting the reservation. All earlier rarity painters and gameplay state remain unchanged.
